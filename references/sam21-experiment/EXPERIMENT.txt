SAM 2.1 MLX — isolated curling experiment

Source: November 6, 2026, Friday 6:15 Mixed League, Koski vs Tillisch, Sheet 1.
Existing local 1080p60 recording reused and its SHA-256 verified against the catalog.
Only read-only SQLite queries were used. No production jobs, dependencies, source files,
reviews, or database records were changed by this experiment.

Runtime: Apple M5 Pro GPU, 64 GiB unified memory; SAM 2.1 Base Plus MLX.
Vendor: https://github.com/eisneim/sam2.1_mlx
Revision: 036f2d02a9069871fd2bf47109ff3b522cd222c4
Weights: https://huggingface.co/eisneim/sam2.1_mlx
Strict parameter loading succeeds; installed versions are in requirements-frozen.txt.

Method: Three eight-second clips, original 60 fps. Each full camera-crop clip is
loaded in memory. One manually inspected positive point on the stone body initializes
each independent track. No corrective prompts are added later. The model uses its
own predicted-quality ranking to choose the mask, matching the vendor demo.
Completed image-feature tensors are discarded; initial conditioning memory and the
16 most recent object-pointer slots are preserved. This bounds cached GPU state
without changing the model's forward memory selection. Vendor files are unchanged.

Outputs per clip: side-by-side source and green mask overlay, mask video, per-frame
pixel observations, sample images, and manifest with runtime and frame counts.
Mask quality scores are uncalibrated. Pixel centroids are NOT calibrated stone
positions. This trial does not reconstruct a complete shot across cameras or establish
whole-game tracking accuracy. Representative means selected illustrative cases,
not a statistically representative evaluation set. Two targets are yellow and one red.

Rerun from repository root (Apple GPU access required):
nice -n 10 data/experiments/sam21-mlx/venv/bin/python -u data/experiments/sam21-mlx/run_experiment.py

The entire compressed source is about 2 GB; full decoded RGB at 60 fps would be
about 2.8 TB, before model preprocessing. Complete individual clips fit in memory.
